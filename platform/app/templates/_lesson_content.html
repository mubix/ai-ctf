You crossed the instruction boundary

The assistant put a private value into its response. Your message came from a customer, yet it overrode the instruction to keep that value private.

Where the attack entered
Your message: this was direct prompt injection.
What failed
The application put a secret in model context and relied on the model to withhold it.
A stronger boundary
Keep secrets out of model context when its job does not need them. Enforce permissions in application code before returning sensitive data.

A longer “never reveal this” instruction is not an access-control check. One successful or refused prompt does not prove how every model will behave.

Reflect on your attempt

What did your message claim the assistant should do? Which part conflicted with its job? How would you check that a legitimate customer can still get help after a fix?
